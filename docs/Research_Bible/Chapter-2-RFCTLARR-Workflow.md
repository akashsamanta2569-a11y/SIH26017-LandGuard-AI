The **RFCTLARR Act, 2013** (Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement Act) is a legal framework in India that governs how the government can acquire private land for public projects. It is designed to balance the state's power to acquire land with strict measures to protect the rights of landowners and displaced families through **social accountability, public consent, fair compensation, and mandatory resettlement**. 

The complete land acquisition lifecycle is structured into **seven sequential phases**:

### Phase 1: Social Impact Assessment (SIA) Phase
Before any land is legally touched, the government must study how the proposed project will affect the local community:
* **SIA Study**: An independent study is launched to evaluate whether the project's public benefits genuinely outweigh its social costs, and to map out how many families will lose their homes or livelihoods.
* **Public Hearing**: The study team holds open, local meetings in the affected areas to record public concerns and objections directly from the residents.
* **Expert Review**: An independent expert committee reviews the final SIA report to give or deny clearance.

### Phase 2: Preliminary Notification Phase
Once the social study is approved, the official legal process begins:
* **Section 11 Notice**: The government publishes a formal notification in local newspapers and the official gazette, declaring that specific land areas are needed for a public purpose.
* **Land Freeze**: The moment this notice is published, landowners are legally restricted from selling, transferring, or developing the marked land.
* **Objection Filing**: Affected landowners are given exactly **60 days** to submit written objections regarding land boundaries, measurements, or the necessity of the project.

### Phase 3: Consent and Survey Phase
This step determines whether the local population agrees to the project and records physical details of the land:
* **Consent Collection**: 
  * For **private sector projects**, the government must get the written consent of at least **80%** of affected families.
  * For **Public-Private Partnerships (PPP)**, at least **70%** consent is required.
  * For **purely government-owned projects**, no public consent is required.
* **Land Survey**: Government survey teams physically inspect the property, update local records, mark boundaries, and value structures, trees, or crops standing on the land.

### Phase 4: Rehabilitation & Resettlement (R&R) Scheme
The law mandates that the government cannot displace people without a clear plan to restore their lives:
* **Draft R&R Plan**: A government-appointed Administrator maps out a comprehensive plan detailing new housing, job opportunities, or public infrastructure (like schools and roads) for displaced families.
* **Public Review**: The draft plan is shared with the public, and a dedicated hearing is held to resolve any missing gaps in the relocation and welfare strategy.

### Phase 5: Declaration and Final Award Phase
This is the formal closing of the acquisition agreement:
* **Section 19 Declaration**: The government publishes a final declaration confirming that the land is being formally and permanently acquired.
* **Final Award**: The District Collector passes a final legal judgment detailing the exact compensation payout and specific R&R benefits assigned to each individual landowner.

### Phase 6: Compensation Calculation
The Act mandates generous payment rates to ensure landowners receive fair value:
* **Rural Land**: Landowners are paid **4 times** the determined market value.
  * *Formula*: Market Value × Multiplier of up to 2 × 100% Solatium (an extra mandatory "comfort" payout equal to the compensation amount).
* **Urban Land**: Landowners are paid **2 times** the determined market value.
  * *Formula*: Market Value × Multiplier of 1 × 100% Solatium.

### Phase 7: Possession Phase
The final hand-off of the property:
* **Payment Clearance**: The government has a strict timeline to pay the full cash compensation within **3 months** of the final award, and complete all R&R monetary benefits within **6 months**.
* **Taking Control**: The government is legally prohibited from taking physical possession of the land until the entire financial payout has been cleared and alternative housing (if relocation was required) is fully built and handed over.
